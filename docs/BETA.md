# Public beta: learning from real use

The first audience is people who prefer listening to everyday post or find small printed text difficult to read. The initial study should establish whether Penny helps with this one task before expanding the product.

## First cohort

Recruit five consenting adult testers through personal introductions or communities that allow research invitations. Include people who use magnification or screen readers where possible. Ask permission before approaching a group; do not imply an endorsement or partnership. There are no claimed participants or measured outcomes yet.

Offer a short session using a synthetic library letter first. A person can decline, skip a question or stop at any time. Do not request banking credentials, sensitive documents or diagnoses. Record observations only with permission, using anonymous participant codes. Do not record faces, screens or audio by default.

## Fifteen-minute session

1. Ask how they currently handle printed post and what is difficult.
2. Ask them to find the sample reader without guidance.
3. Ask them to read aloud, stop, and download the text.
4. Offer a synthetic paper letter to photograph. Observe framing, waiting and error recovery.
5. Ask them to explain where their photo went and what the AI consent control does.
6. Ask what would make them use Penny again, and what would stop them.

## Evidence to collect

| Question                        | Measure                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Can people start independently? | Number completing the first sample reading without assistance                                 |
| Is OCR useful?                  | Successful readings / attempted readings, with device and document conditions                 |
| Are the controls accessible?    | Blockers encountered with keyboard, magnification or screen reader                            |
| Is the data flow clear?         | Correct explanation of local reading versus optional AI sharing                               |
| Do people return?               | Voluntary follow-up after seven days: used again, task performed, reason for returning or not |

Keep findings in a private study log. Publish only aggregate observations with permission and sample size. Separate visits, testers and returning users; none is interchangeable. Do not add user counts, testimonials or retention claims before collecting evidence.

## After each session

Create a reproducible issue using synthetic content, fix the highest-impact blocker, and invite the participant to retry only if they opted into follow-up. Link shipped improvements to the relevant issue. A small number of people returning for a useful task is more informative than a large launch-view count.
